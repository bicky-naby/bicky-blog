Now before we get into the SCOTTISH DRAMZ let me give you the facts 💁‍♀️. This is a **1999 thriller** written and directed by **Anthony Neilson**, a Scottish dramatist who decided to make a movie about forgiveness and revenge and ALSO about the macho culture of modern urban Scottish life 🏴󠁧󠁢󠁳󠁣󠁴󠁿. Starring **Billy Connolly** as Nicky Dryden (the ex-criminal turn sculptor), **Ken Stott** as Gary Keltie (the cop who's like "YOU NEVER PAID YOUR DEBT TO SOCIETY" and literally stalks him to his art show 😤), and **Francesca Annis** as Val, the wife who's like "um honey you never told me you used to beat up people's relatives??" and he's like "it was my POLICY babe" and she's like **sits down slowly** O_o.

okay but the STRANGEST thing about this whole movie is the **documented specifics**. So in the 1970s, Dryden is arrested for enforcing loanshark debts in Edinburgh. Then he gets out of prison, goes clean, and becomes a **feted sculptor** showing his first exhibition 🖼️. And then Keltie shows up at HIS ART SHOW and is DISGUSTED by Dryden's new-found respectability. Like bro let him have his art show moment??? He made a sculpture??? Let him COOK? But Keltie is like "YOU MUST PAY YOUR DEBT TO ME" and it's this whole psychological cat-and-mouse thing where Dryden confesses to his wife that his "policy" was to intimidate debtors by **assaulting their close relatives** 😳 which is HORRIFYING but ALSO like, that's how you're gonna be a sculptor now? with those hands?? **looks at my own hands** they could never.

AND THEN there's a young wannabe gangster named **Flipper** who is OBSESSED with Dryden's dark past and wants to be just like him and escalates his crimes to MURDER at a swimming pool and I'm like **eating popcorn with one hand, covering my eyes with the other** 🍿👁️ — this movie is A LOT and I haven't EVEN seen it yet.
